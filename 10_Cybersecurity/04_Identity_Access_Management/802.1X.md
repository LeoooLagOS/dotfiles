# 802.1X

## 📝 One-Sentence Summary
*IEEE 802.1X is a [[Port-based Network Access Control (PNAC)]] standard that authenticates devices or users before granting them access to a LAN through a switch port.*
## 💡 Analogy
*Think of 802.1X as a **hotel key card system for network ports***. 
* When you plug your laptop into the wall jack (the switch port), you need to present your key card (credentials). 
* The front desk (authentication server) verifies your card before activating the port and letting you into your room (the network).
## 🔑 Key Details
- **What it is:** 
	- A port-based access control and authentication protocol defined by the [[IEEE 802.1 standard]]. 
- **Why it exists:** 
	- To restrict unauthorized devices (workstations, printers, etc.) from connecting to publicly accessible switch ports in a LAN. 
	- The authentication server validates the client before allowing access to LAN services. 
- **Roles:** 
	* **Supplicant (Client):** 
		* The device requesting access (e.g., a user's laptop running 802.1X software). 
	* **Authenticator (Switch):** 
		* The network device controlling physical access (e.g., the switch). It acts as an intermediary, relaying information between the supplicant and the authentication server. 
	* **Authentication Server:** 
		* The central device (typically [[RADIUS]]) that validates the supplicant's credentials and tells the authenticator whether to grant or deny access.
## 🔗 Connections & Implementations
* 802.1X is a common method for implementing the authentication part of [[AAA|AAA]] and [[NAC|NAC]] solutions.
