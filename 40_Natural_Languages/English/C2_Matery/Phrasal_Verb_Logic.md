# Phrasal Verb Logic: The Particle Theory

## 📝 One-Sentence Summary
Phrasal verbs are not random; the meaning is usually dictated by the **Particle** (Up, Down, Off), which describes the "State Change" of the object, regardless of the verb used.

## 💡 Core Rule & Analogy
**The "State Machine" Analogy:**
Think of the Particle as the **State Transition** function.
* **Verb:** The method of execution (Run, Turn, Cut).
* **Particle:** The final state of the system (Completed, Disconnected, Reduced).

## ⚙️ The Big Three Particles

### 1. UP = Completion / Limit Reached
* **Logic:** The container is full. The process is 100% done.
* **Examples:**
    * *Drink* vs *Drink **up*** (Finish your glass).
    * *Clean* vs *Clean **up*** (Make it spotless).
    * *Use* vs *Use **up*** (Deplete resources to 0).
    * *Wrap* vs *Wrap **up*** (Finish the project).

### 2. DOWN = Stabilization / Reduction / Fixation
* **Logic:** Energy is lost, movement stops, or things are pinned to the ground.
* **Examples:**
    * *Calm **down*** (Reduce energy).
    * *Pin **down*** (Fix a bug/person so they can't move/avoid).
    * *Track **down*** (Find something hidden).
    * *Die **down*** (Noise/Signal fading away).

### 3. OFF = Disconnection / Departure
* **Logic:** A clean break from a surface or state. Active $\to$ Inactive.
* **Examples:**
    * *Call **off*** (Cancel/Disconnect the event).
    * *Cut **off*** (Sever the link).
    * *Log **off*** (Leave the system).
    * *Doze **off*** (Disconnect from consciousness/sleep).

## 🚀 C2 Precision Test
* "The fire burned." (Fact)
* "The fire burned **down**." (It lost energy and stopped).
* "The fire burned **up**." (It consumed the fuel completely).
* "The fire burned **out**." (It exhausted itself and died).
