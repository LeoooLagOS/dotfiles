# C2 Nuance: Hedging & Indirectness

## 📝 One-Sentence Summary
Mastery of English involves "Hedging"—using specific modifiers to soften statements, add plausible deniability, or convey irony without changing the literal meaning.

## 💡 Core Rule: The "Directness" Spectrum
* **A1-B2 (Direct):** "This is wrong." (High risk of conflict).
* **C1-C2 (Hedged):** "That’s not entirely accurate." (Low risk, professional).

## ⚙️ Syntactical Tools

### 1. The "Seem/Appear" Filter
Instead of stating facts, state *perceptions*.
* *Direct:* "The server crashed."
* *C2:* "The server **seems to have** crashed."
* *C2:* "It **appears** we have a latency issue."

### 2. The "Not Quite/Entirely" Softener
Negating the absolute.
* *Direct:* "I disagree."
* *C2:* "I don't **entirely** agree with that premise."
* *C2:* "That's **not exactly** what I meant."

### 3. Understatement (Litotes)
Using a negative to express a positive (very common in British English).
* *Meaning:* "It was a disaster."
* *C2 Output:* "It wasn't exactly a triumph."
* *Meaning:* "It is expensive."
* *C2 Output:* "It's not cheap."

## 🚀 Examples in Context (Code Review)
* *Bad (Too blunt):* "Your code is messy."
* *C2 Professional:* "The code **could be a bit** cleaner in this module."
* *C2 Irony:* "It's certainly a **creative** solution." (Translation: It's weird).
