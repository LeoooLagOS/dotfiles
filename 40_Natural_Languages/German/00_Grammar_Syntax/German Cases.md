# German Cases (Nominative, Accusative, Dative)

## 📝 One-Sentence Summary
The "Case" system assigns a specific role (Subject, Direct Object, Indirect Object) to every noun, changing its article (der/die/das) to signal that role.

## 💡 Core Rule & Analogy
**Analogy: Software Permissions**
* **Nominative (Admin):** The User who *executes* the command. (The Subject).
* **Accusative (File):** The file *being deleted/opened*. (The Direct Object).
* **Dative (Recipient):** The person you are *sending* the file to. (The Indirect Object).

## ⚙️ Syntactical Structure (The Change Table)

| Case | Role | Question | Masc (Der) | Fem (Die) | Neut (Das) | Plural (Die) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative** | Subject | Wer/Was? | **Der** | Die | Das | Die |
| **Accusative** | Direct Obj | Wen/Was? | **Den** (Change!) | Die | Das | Die |
| **Dative** | Indirect Obj | Wem? | **Dem** (Change!) | **Der** (Change!) | **Dem** (Change!) | **Den + n** |

## 🚀 Examples in Context
1.  **Nominative:** *Der Mann* isst. (The man eats.)
2.  **Accusative:** Der Mann isst *den Apfel*. (The man eats the apple.)
3.  **Dative:** Der Mann gibt *dem Kind* den Apfel. (The man gives the apple *to the child*.)
