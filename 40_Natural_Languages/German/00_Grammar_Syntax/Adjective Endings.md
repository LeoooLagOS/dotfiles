# Adjective Endings

## 📝 One-Sentence Summary
German adjective endings change based on whether the preceding article already indicates the gender and case of the noun.

## 💡 Core Rule & Analogy
**"Capture the Flag" (Signal Theory):**
German grammar demands that the Case (Nom/Akk/Dat) and Gender (Der/Die/Das) must be shown **at least once** in the noun phrase.
1.  **Lazy Adjective:** If the Article carries the flag (shows the signal), the adjective can relax (ends in `-e` or `-en`).
2.  **Strong Adjective:** If the Article is missing or weak (like "ein"), the adjective must carry the flag itself (e.g., *ein gut**er*** Mann).

## ⚙️ Syntactical Structure

### Scenario A: Definite Articles (The "Der/Die/Das" Table)
*The article has the signal. Adjective is lazy.*

| Case | Maskulin | Feminin | Neutral | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nom** | der gut**e** | die gut**e** | das gut**e** | die gut**en** |
| **Akk** | den gut**en** | die gut**e** | das gut**e** | die gut**en** |
| **Dat** | dem gut**en** | der gut**en** | dem gut**en** | den gut**en** |

### Scenario B: Indefinite Articles (The "Ein/Eine" Table)
*The article is ambiguous. Adjective must work.*

| Case | Maskulin | Feminin | Neutral | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nom** | ein gut**er** | eine gut**e** | ein gut**es** | keine gut**en** |
| **Akk** | einen gut**en** | eine gut**e** | ein gut**es** | keine gut**en** |
| **Dat** | einem gut**en** | einer gut**en** | einem gut**en** | keinen gut**en** |

## 🚀 Examples in Context
**Maskulin Nominativ:**
* *Der* gut**e** Mann (Article shows "Der" → Adjective is lazy).
* *Ein* gut**er** Mann (Article "ein" is unclear → Adjective shows "Der").

**Neutral Akkusativ:**
* *Das* schnell**e** Auto.
* *Ein* schnell**es** Auto (Adjective must show "Das").
