# Subordinate Clauses (Nebensätze)

## 📝 One-Sentence Summary
Subordinate clauses are dependent sentences introduced by specific connectors (like *weil*, *dass*, *wenn*) that force the conjugated verb to move from position 2 to the very end of the sentence.

## 💡 Core Rule & Analogy
**The "Verb Kicker" Analogy:**
Imagine the connector word is a football player. As soon as words like *weil* or *dass* enter the field, they kick the verb (the ball) all the way to the goal line (the end of the sentence).
* **Main Clause:** The verb sits comfortably in the VIP lounge (Position 2).
* **Subordinate Clause:** The verb is kicked to the exit.

## ⚙️ Syntactical Structure
**The Formula:**
`[Main Clause] , [Connector] + [Subject] + [Objects/Time/Place] + [Verb] .`

**The Connectors (Triggers):**
* **weil** (because) → Reason
* **dass** (that) → Object
* **wenn** (if/when) → Condition
* **ob** (whether) → Indirect Yes/No Question

## 🚀 Examples in Context
**1. Reason (weil)**
* *Wrong:* ... weil der Parkplatz **war** voll.
* *Right:* Ich bin zu Fuß gegangen, **weil** der Parkplatz voll **war**.

**2. Object (dass)**
* *Wrong:* Ich weiß, dass du **bist** klug.
* *Right:* Ich weiß, **dass** du klug **bist**.

**3. Separable Verbs (The Sandwich)**
With verbs like *aufstehen*, the prefix and the verb stick together at the end.
* *Right:* ... weil ich jeden Tag um 6 Uhr **aufstehe**.
