# Possessive Articles (Possessivartikel)

## 📝 One-Sentence Summary
Possessive articles indicate ownership (my, your, his) and act as **"Ein-Words"**, meaning they strictly inherit the exact same grammatical endings as the indefinite article (*ein*).

## 💡 Core Rule & Analogy
Think of a Possessive Article as a two-part variable: **`$Stem + $Ending`**.

1.  **The Stem (Who owns it?):** This is constant. (e.g., `mein-`, `dein-`, `sein-`).
2.  **The Ending (The Case/Gender):** This is dynamic and determined by the object being owned.

**The Algorithm:**
> If you know the ending for **Ein**, you just copy/paste it to the Stem.
> * *Ein* Mann $\to$ *Mein* Mann.
> * *Ein****em*** Mann $\to$ *Mein****em*** Mann.

## ⚙️ Syntactical Structure

### 1. Select the Stem (The Owner)
| Person | German Stem | English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ich** | **mein-** | my |
| **Du** | **dein-** | your (singular) |
| **Er / Es** | **sein-** | his / its |
| **Sie** | **ihr-** | her |
| **Wir** | **unser-** | our |
| **Ihr** | **euer-** | your (plural) |
| **Sie / sie**| **ihr- / Ihr-**| their / Your (formal) |

### 2. Apply the Ending (The Logic Table)
Add these endings to the stem selected above.

| Case | Masculine (Der) | Neuter (Das) | Feminine (Die) | Plural (Die) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nominative**<br>*(Subject)* | --<br>*(mein)* | --<br>*(mein)* | **-e**<br>*(meine)* | **-e**<br>*(meine)* |
| **Accusative**<br>*(Direct Object)* | **-en**<br>*(meinen)* | --<br>*(mein)* | **-e**<br>*(meine)* | **-e**<br>*(meine)* |
| **Dative**<br>*(Receiver/Static)*| **-em**<br>*(meinem)* | **-em**<br>*(meinem)* | **-er**<br>*(meiner)* | **-en** (+n)<br>*(meinen ...n)* |

> **⚠️ Exception:** For **euer** (our), if the ending is **-en** or **-em**, we usually drop the internal 'e' for flow.
> * *Euer* $\to$ *Eur****em*** (Not *Euerem*).

## 🚀 Examples in Context

**1. Masculine (Der Computer)**
* **Nom:** **Mein** Computer ist neu. (Subject $\to$ No ending)
* **Acc:** Ich brauche **meinen** Computer. (Direct Object $\to$ **-en**)
* **Dat:** Ich arbeite an **meinem** Computer. (Static Location $\to$ **-em**)

**2. Feminine (Die Schwester)**
* **Nom:** **Meine** Schwester ist hier. (Subject $\to$ **-e**)
* **Acc:** Ich rufe **meine** Schwester an. (Direct Object $\to$ **-e**)
* **Dat:** Ich danke **meiner** Schwester. (Dative Verb $\to$ **-er**)

**3. Plural (Die Freunde)**
* **Dat:** Ich gehe mit **meinen** Freunde**n**. (Dative Plural $\to$ **-en** + noun **n**)
