# _Computer Science_

## 📝 One-Sentence Summary
Computer Science is the formal study of computation, information, and automation, and how these principles can be applied to solve complex problems.

## 💡 Analogy
This folder is the **Physics department** of the university. It doesn't build the engines ([[_Programming Languages]]) or the cars (`30_Projects/`), but it discovers and defines the fundamental laws (like gravity and thermodynamics) that make all engineering possible.

## 📜 History & Creator
While its roots go back to mathematics and early calculators, modern Computer Science was born from the work of **Alan Turing** in the 1930s (defining computability with the [[Turing Machine]]) and **John von Neumann** (designing the [[Von Neumann Architecture]]). It was formalized as its own academic discipline in the 1950s and 60s.
## 🔑 Core Philosophy & Design
The core philosophy of Computer Science is **abstraction**. It is the art of building complex, reliable, and efficient systems by creating layers of abstraction—from the pure mathematics of computation up to the logical and physical implementation.

## ⚙️ Key Details
-   **What it is:** 
	- The academic discipline studying the theory, design, and application of computers and computational systems.
-   **Why it exists:** 
	- To formalize how we solve problems using logic, algorithms, and automation.
-   **Core Sub-Pillars:** This folder organizes the most abstract theories:
    -   **`_Foundations/`**: The basic building blocks of programming, like [[Variables and Memory]] and [[Data Types]].
    -   **`_Theory_of_Computation/`**: The formal mathematics of what is computable.

## 🔗 Connections & Implementations
The principles in this folder are the bedrock for all other domains in this vault.
-   The concepts in `_Foundations/` are used to build [[_Programming Languages]].
-   The principles in `_Theory_of_Computation/` define the limits and efficiency of [[_Algorithms]].
-   This entire field provides the logical basis for [[_Operating Systems]] and [[_Computer Architecture]].
