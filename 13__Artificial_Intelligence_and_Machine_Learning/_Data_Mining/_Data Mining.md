# _Data Mining

## 📝 One-Sentence Summary
The process of discovering hidden patterns, anomalies, and valid correlations within large datasets to predict outcomes or simplify complex information.

## 💡 Analogy
Data Mining is like being a digital gold prospector: you sift through tons of raw "dirt" (unstructured data) using specialized tools (algorithms) to find the rare, valuable "nuggets" (insights) that were previously invisible.

## 📜 History & Creator
While the practice dates back to [[Bayes' Theorem]] (1763) and [[Regression]] (1805), the term "Data Mining" emerged in the 1990s database community. Key figures include Gregory Piatetsky-Shapiro (who co-founded KDD).

## 🔑 Core Philosophy & Design
The philosophy centers on **[[KDD|Knowledge Discovery in Databases (KDD)]]**. It is an iterative process where the goal is not just to collect data, but to transform it into actionable intelligence.

## ⚙️ Key Details
- **What it is:** A subfield of CS involving intersection of statistics, machine learning, and database systems.
- **Why it exists:** To bridge the gap between "Big Data" and "Big Knowledge."
- **Core Functions/Components:**
	- **Selection:** Identifying the target data.
	- **Preprocessing/Cleaning:** Removing "noise."
	- **Transformation:** Normalizing data for processing.
	- **Data Mining:** Applying the actual algorithm (Clustering, Association, etc.).
	- **Interpretation/Evaluation:** Visualizing results to find meaning.

## 🔗 Connections & Implementations
- **Business Intelligence:** Predicting customer churn.
- **Fraud Detection:** Identifying suspicious banking patterns.
- **Scientific Research:** Genomic sequencing and protein folding patterns.
